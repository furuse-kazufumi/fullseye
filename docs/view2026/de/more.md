- [A dent invisible in a diffusely lit photo appears in a fringe mirror image (synthetic)](../../articles/assets/poc/poc_phase_measuring_deflectometry/03_photo_vs_pmd.png) (Abbildung): A very shallow dent in glossy paint barely shows in a photograph under diffuse light. Projecting fringes on a screen facing the surface and reading how their mirror image bends gives curvature, and a planted dent 3 µm deep stands out. This is a synthetic scene, and photographs under oblique light were not compared.<a id="d-poc-phase-measuring-deflectometry"></a>
  A dent a few micrometres deep in a glossy surface (a painted car panel, a cover glass) is all but invisible in a lit photograph, because a mirror only shows the light. Put a screen in front of the surface, show phase-shifted fringes and photograph their mirror image: a tilt of 1e-3 rad moves the reflected point 0.6 mm on a screen 30 cm away. Six new ops (`deflectometry`, category `deflectometry` of `opsspecular`): the rig, the screen pattern, the forward render (height field to fringe mirror image, with a diffuse fraction, scatter, the back reflection of glass, aperture-integrated focus and noise), phase to normals (via `match3d.normal_from_reflection`), slope to curvature (one derivative, no integration) and the closed-form sensitivity.  **On a painted panel with 3 µm and 1 µm dents, 0.8 µm waviness and 0.12 µm polishing marks the photograph's defect signal peaks at 0.00066 (0.17 of one 8-bit step) while the curvature map shows the dents at SNR 110 and 57, and the integrated depth is within 0.5 % of the truth. Phase noise matches sqrt(2/N) sigma/b at 1.00 and slope noise is 0.67 of `sigma_u`/(2d), the sqrt(4/9) of bilinear resampling. Where it breaks: lifting the part by 0.3 mm adds the apparent tilt dz sin(theta)/d (to 0.1 %) and a 388 m bowl while the dent depth moves 0.7 %; a 2 % screen-distance error makes dents 2.3 % shallower; a 1 µm dent survives a diffuse fraction of 0.85 and fringe orders start to jump at 0.9; on uncoated cover glass the back-surface light is bent the other way by the dent's wedge, so the slope sensitivity shrinks to the closed-form 0.246 (measured 0.246) and dents look a quarter as deep; with an 8 mm aperture, focusing on the surface leaves 0.60 of the fringe amplitude and focusing on the screen leaves 0.52 of the depth. Nothing learned, nothing calibrated, 22 gates.** [Quelle](https://github.com/furuse-kazufumi/fullseye/blob/master/examples/poc_phase_measuring_deflectometry.py)
- [Lackschichtdicke ohne Schnitt (industrielle OCT)](../../articles/assets/poc/poc_industrial_oct/01_real_paint_bscans.png) (Abbildung): We want to see paint and film layers in depth without cutting. Clear-coat thicknesses from public raw interference spectra of automotive paint agree within 3.6 µm with microscope measurements of the same panels. Agreement with the paper's own values only reproduces the same data, and the panel matching is inferred.<a id="d-poc-industrial-oct"></a>
  Optical coherence tomography sees the layer stack of paints, conformal coatings, adhesive layers and multilayer films, and voids and delaminations inside them, without cutting. Thirteen new ops (`octscan`, category `oct` of `opsoptics`): closed-form design numbers before measuring, interferogram simulators driven by the material table, k-linearisation, dispersion compensation and estimation, A- and B-scans, layer thickness through the group index (refusing layers that cannot be separated), boundary tracing, surface flattening and an entry point for raw spectra.  **Real data: public raw spectra of automotive paint panels (CC BY 4.0) processed with Fullseye ops alone give clear-coat thicknesses within 1.5 µm of the paper's OCT and 3.6 µm of the microscope, base coats within 2.3 µm of the microscope for three of four panels (one is 7.6 µm off and unresolved), a refusal under the white base coat, and an autocorrelation ghost lying flat at the clear coat's optical thickness. Synthetic: thickness through the group index within 0.04 µm of the truth, SNR, roll-off and dispersion broadening matching the closed forms, refusal below 1.4 point-spread widths, a coating-thickness map and void/delamination detection.** [Quelle](https://github.com/furuse-kazufumi/fullseye/blob/master/examples/poc_industrial_oct.py)
  Terms: OCT — A non-contact method that uses low-coherence interference to measure depth-resolved reflectivity and form cross-sectional images.

Scored per pixel on separate captures, most of it was detected at 1.1 % false alarms. With fewer bands the false alarms grew, and that a colour camera fails on same-coloured matter is shown only on synthetic data.<a id="d-poc-hyperspectral-foreign-matter"></a>

A library from a separate scan of reference samples separates PP / PET / PE at 0.878; this PoC's material-table physics alone gives 0.628 (chance 0.333). Black plastic reflects 0.03 with a C-H trough one sixth as deep. The test header still carried 450 fwhm values for 360 bands and the fail-closed reader refused it; the wavelength labels were verified on the polymer troughs and only fwhm was fixed in a copy.  **Foreign objects on pork belly, ten classes (Zenodo 17242553, an InGaAs line-scan spectral camera): split by capture, 656k test pixels; SAM gives AUC 0.92 with 10 % false alarms, LDA on all 184 bands detects 0.92 at 1.1 % false alarms, six Fisher bands give 2.1 % and three give 23 % — three bands sufficed in simulation but not on real data. The core is scoring on other people's measured data (both CC BY 4.0). In the microspectroscopy of Schwarz et al. (2024) the measured reflectance of bare silicon agrees with the table's silicon to 0.4 % (median); a normal-incidence fit gives 92.9 nm against the 98.5 nm reference (−5.7 %), and choosing the NA by the residual (0.5) gives 95.8 nm (−2.7 %); the coverage of the measurement spot is read back over 11 spectra with a mean error of 0.6 points. - [Magnification measured on public telecentric images agrees with the paper within the paper's own spread](../../articles/assets/poc/poc_telecentric_metrology/08_real_alignment_plate.png) (Abbildung): In one image, a stepped part looks larger where it is nearer through an ordinary lens. The magnification measured on public real images from a telecentric lens agrees with the paper, whose own values spread by ±0.25 %, so the match is not read as finer. Ring centres on a tilted plate that do not drift are evidence.<a id="d-poc-telecentric-metrology"></a>
  The lead chapter measures the paper's raw telecentric images (Stark et al., Sci. Rep. 15, 28690, 2025, CC BY 4.0) with Fullseye's metrology ops and checks them against the paper: a family of ellipses sharing one centre and shape, fitted to the eight rings of an alignment plate (1 mm spacing) tilted by 29.5°, gives 252.13 ± 0.04 px/mm along the major axis, +0.05 % from the paper's K1 = 252.0 px/mm (the paper's own K1 and K2 spread by ±0.25 %, so the match is not read as finer than that). The telecentric signature: the centres of the concentric rings on the tilted plate do not drift with r² (β = 0.0035 ± 0.0034 px/mm², where a 25 mm entocentric lens would give 1.40 and move the outer centre by 101 px), so the entrance pupil lies beyond 10.5 m. Fitting a free ellipse to each ring instead fails on the outer rings whose arcs are shadowed down to 36–180°. A 3D-printed resin sphere (nominal radius 2.5 mm) is not a usable reference: its outline radius moves 8 % with the threshold. The synthetic chapters that follow sweep the failure conditions. Die bonding and connector-pin inspection measure features at different heights in one image. An ordinary (entocentric) lens magnifies nearer faces more, so the same dimension reads differently above and below a step; a telecentric lens puts the stop at a focal point so the chief rays run parallel to the axis. Seven new ops (`telecentric`, category `telecentric` of `opsoptics`): the same BK7-class singlet (f = 100 mm) with only the stop moved (entocentric, object-side and bi-telecentric), the real chief ray found by aiming at the stop itself so it works with the entrance pupil at infinity (where `raytrace.chief_ray` stops), magnification versus height, the error across a step, defocus blur, vignetting and a pinhole camera at the entrance pupil for `render3d`. Moving the object 5 mm closer on a fixed image plane changes the magnification by +6.9 % for a 25 mm machine-vision lens at 75 mm, +1.7 % for the 100 mm entocentric lens and −0.013 % for the telecentric ones; the real rays agree with L/(L − Δz) to 7e-5, and refocusing does not help (m = −f/(s − f) moves by +2.6 %). Rendered through the three lenses, a 5 mm pin top reads 6.9 % too large with the short lens (closed form to 7e-4) and its reading moves 8.6 % over a 6 mm stroke; 2,053 pixels of the bore wall show and only 0.948 of the hole bottom is visible (circle-overlap closed form 0.949), while the bi-telecentric view sees the whole bottom and no wall. The parts are rendered with real material appearance (a brushed aluminium fixture plate, a gold-plated pin, a black moulded package with tinned leads; machining marks and metal colours from complex refractive indices, a clear-coat resin, shadows and ambient occlusion), and under coaxial, dome and low-angle ring lighting a circle metrology model applied to the rendered image reads the pin top within 0.33 % of the closed form, +8.3 to 8.4 % across a 6 mm stroke for the short lens and within ±0.16 % for the bi-telecentric one; at a coarser 47 µm per pixel the low-angle ring reading drifts by 2 % because the dark-field edge is weak. What remains: the singlet's pupil spherical aberration leaves 0.05° at 8 mm field (growing as h³) and 3 % distortion for the object-side design (0.005 % bi-telecentric); a stop shift moves the entrance pupil to −f²/δ as Newton's equation says (0.04 % at 0.5 mm field, 11.8 % more at 8 mm); a residual 0.1° shifts a point 9.2 µm across a 5 mm step (closed form 8.7 µm). The front lens sets the field: the 50 % point at 11.6 mm falls 0.8 mm short of the paraxial overlap because pupil aberration shifts and widens the bundle. The blur slope matches |m|·NA/√2 within 0.5 %, and NA 0.033 (the smallest that resolves 22 µm) caps the depth of field at 0.68 mm.  **A second implementation, the open-source optiland, gives the same chief-ray image height to 9e-8 mm (with the same index from a public glass catalogue, so it checks the tracing and aiming code). Nothing learned, nothing calibrated, 29 gates.** [Quelle](https://github.com/furuse-kazufumi/fullseye/blob/master/examples/poc_telecentric_metrology.py)
- [Virtual SEM: the image changes with voltage (model scored against measured backscatter)](../../articles/assets/poc/poc_virtual_sem_kv_cd_bse/05_sem_same_part_3kv_3detectors.png) (Abbildung): An SEM image is not a photo of the surface but a tally of electrons escaping after scattering in the solid. A model that tracks each electron was checked against measured backscatter data for 7 elements. With no real images of known linewidth, the shift of the linewidth reading with beam energy stays inside the model.<a id="d-poc-virtual-sem-kv-cd-bse"></a>
  An SEM image is not a photograph of the surface: it is a tally of what comes out of the interaction volume of primary electrons scattering inside the solid. Eight new ops (`sem_electron_mc`, `sem_yield_curve`, `sem_se_universal_curve`, `sem_line_profile`, `sem_cd_measure`, `sem_image`, `sem_charging_series`, `sem_se_escape_fraction`) and the material-table op `material_electron_properties` build a Monte Carlo that tracks electrons one by one (Joy's single scattering, Browning's 1994 Mott fit for elastic scattering, the Joy-Luo stopping power), and every stage is scored against outside measurements. The backscatter coefficient for 7 elements (C to Au) at 5/10/20/30 keV falls inside the measured bands of Joy's database plus or minus 0.04; switching back to screened Rutherford fails for W and Au. The maximum penetration is 0.83 to 1.04 of the Kanaya-Okayama range and the depth grows as E^1.75 (Si) and E^1.52 (Au) against 1.67. The shape of the secondary-electron yield matches the Lin and Joy 2005 universal curve within RMS 0.076 for C to Ag, with Emax within -4 to +7 % of their table (not fitted); W and Au have heavier tails (SE2 dominates, unresolved). The user's guess that W on Si is invisible in SE but stands out in backscatter Z contrast holds only for flush W at 1 kV (SE +5 %, BSE +96 %); at 5 kV SE shows it too (+80 %) because the SE2 made by exiting backscattered electrons carries Z. W buried 150 nm deep shows +3 % at 1 kV even in backscatter and +154 % at 20 kV (information depth). For CD-SEM, a PMMA line (40 nm at the base) with an 88 degree sidewall measures 39.1 nm at 1 kV and 39.9 nm at 20 kV by threshold: low voltage measures high on the sidewall (0.33), high voltage at the foot (0.03); at 80 degrees the difference is small and reversed, so this is no general rule.  **A vertical sidewall reads wide at every voltage (+0.3 to +1.0 nm). Charging: at 0.5 kV (eta + delta > 1) the insulator charges positive, darkens and stops at the potential that solves eta + delta F(V*) = 1 by an independent bisection (to 2e-15 V); at 20 kV it keeps charging negative and brightens (phenomenological). A defect found on the way is kept on record: free paths were drawn in the starting material and walked straight through material boundaries, so a 0.01 nm Si cap changed W's eta from 0.425 to 0.329 (now truncated at the boundary, with a regression gate). No real-image chapter yet: no CC BY CD-SEM images with ground-truth line widths were found.** [Quelle](https://github.com/furuse-kazufumi/fullseye/blob/master/examples/poc_virtual_sem_kv_cd_bse.py)

- [Fehler aus Gutteil-Streuung: die erklärte Fehlalarmrate hält bei echten Bildern nicht](../../articles/assets/poc/poc_good_sample_variation_model/04_visa_explanation.png) (Abbildung): A classic inspection learns the usual per-pixel variation from good parts and flags what falls outside. Here the band is set by a false-alarm rate declared in advance and scored on 200 public real images. Alignment mattered more than the number of good parts, and the declared rate was not kept.<a id="d-poc-good-sample-variation-model"></a>
  The inspection classic, learning every pixel's usual value and spread from many good samples and flagging what leaves the band, as an op family whose band width is derived from a declared per-pixel false-alarm rate instead of being set by hand (five new varmodel_* ops plus four method-independent anomeval metrics). For normal good samples a new pixel's deviation follows a t prediction interval, so the band width is closed-form. False-alarm rates counted on synthetic good samples fall inside the binomial 4-sigma band for per-pixel std, one pooled variance, empirical-Bayes shrinkage (Smyth 2004) and median/MAD, and the pooled variance fails when the noise differs per pixel. With five good samples the std band is 9.4 sigma wide (the t4 tail); shrinkage brings it to 5.0 and lifts the detection of 5-sigma flaws from 0.13 to 0.52. A good set shifted by 1-2 px is refused; forced through, the band on edges grows 22-fold and misses every edge flaw, and registering by a 1/20-px Fourier shift brings it back to 1.07. With three of twenty "good" samples carrying the same flaw, std misses the real flaw there (0.00) while median/MAD finds it (0.98). On the public VisA pcb1 data (CC BY 4.0, 200 test images) the registered band reaches pixel AUROC 0.808 and AUPRO 0.678 against 0.705 and 0.460 unregistered; golden comparison scores a higher pixel AUROC (0.826) but a lower AUPRO (0.519).  **The declared 1e-3 false-alarm rate is not kept on real images (3.8 %), and recalibrating on held-out good images only reaches 2.4 %: a difference between the training and test captures remains that a translation cannot remove.
